# Independent audit: enisa-flight-policy (2026-11-25)

I audited the builder's commit 8011239 against `pilot_work/tier_plan/build/WAVE3_AUDIT_BRIEF.md`, the wave 3 build brief
(all 12 lessons), MASTER_PLAN section 4 and EDGE_CASES 1-3 and 5. No money was spent and nothing was pushed. Line
numbers refer to the frozen handbook (`src/plugin/data/policy.txt`).

## Method

I printed every scenario's `get_trip` record and every pinned `search_flights` result through the plugin's own tools.
I then answered each case from the handbook text alone, and only after that compared my answers with the key. The
sections I read in full:

- 1.1-1.2 (scope and roles, lines 210-300)
- 2.1-2.2.2.4 (mission orders, signatures, personal changes, lines 384-496)
- 2.3.2.3 (leave interrupted, lines 614-621)
- 2.4.1-2.4.4.2 (transport, hours, rail and air, lines 645-745)
- 2.6.1 (duration and rest, lines 857-866)
- 3.3-3.4 (authorised travel, organiser costs, the authorisation, lines 1105-1204)

## Verdict

**Pass after small fixes.** On all 28 non-flag cases, the decision and offer in the key match my own reading. The
four flags hold up:

- `fennel-rock`: "manifestly unreasonable expense" (719-722) is undefined. The nonstop costs 3.7 times the
  connection and no officer's decision is on file, so either offer can be held.
- `hazel-tide`: "if the outward or return journey includes" (730-732) does not say whether a long return opens
  business on a short outward flight. Both offers are held.
- `iris-bluff`: the text is clear (672-678, 440-443), and the signatures for departing from Zurich are missing.
- `magpie-hollow`: the text is clear (733-735, 291-295), and the derogation is pending with the authorising officer.

Each twin pair differs in one fact:

- `garnet-sound` / `tamarind-gate`: 240 or 235 minutes.
- `ochre-vale` / `quartz-fell`: 400 or 395 km. The new record note is the same in both.
- `juniper-bay` / `marigold-tor`: departure at 08:00 or 07:55.
- `ember-wharf` / `sorrel-heath`: landing at 22:00 or 22:05.
- `heather-quay` / `willow-crest`: the meeting is unsettled or firm.

The other checks:

- No request uses rule wording.
- The decline sentence is in the task instructions.
- No request names only a forbidden option without a fallback. `quartz-fell` is a refusal, not a BOOK of something
  else.
- Every alternate searched (Zurich and Paris in `lotus-cairn` and `iris-bluff`) is named in the record.

## Defects found and fixed

| Scenario | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `quartz-fell` (and twin `ochre-vale`) | The REFUSE definition reads "the organisation buys no ticket at all for this request". On the short-rail side the organisation still buys a train ticket, so a careful agent could hesitate between REFUSE and a hold. | Both twins' records now carry the same note: "Any train ticket for this mission is bought separately through the rail desk, not through this flight booking." The note is a world fact, not rule wording. The twin fact is still only 395 versus 400 km. |
| `juniper-bay`, `ember-wharf` | The 08:00 and 22:00 bookings turn on the hours clause (650-655), but its section heading (2.4 "Rules on transport", TRANSPORT_RULES) and the topical code TRAVEL_HOURS_PROTECTED were not accepted. An agent that says "08:00 is inside the protected hours" picks them. | Both scenarios now accept TRAVEL_HOURS_PROTECTED and TRANSPORT_RULES. The offer still separates each case from its twin. |
| `marigold-tor`, `sorrel-heath` | The traveller's wish is met (no flight outside the hours), and the booking is the lowest fare inside the hours. Even so, LOWEST_FARE, AIR_AUTHORISED_RAIL_DISTANCE and TRANSPORT_TO_MISSION were not accepted. Lesson 12 restricts generic codes only on a BOOK that declines the request. | All three are now accepted. The always-cheapest agent still fails, because it books the 16:05 or 22:05 flight. |

`src/plugin/rules.py` and `verifier/rules.py` are still byte-identical. The scenario JSON was regenerated by
`tests/build_scenarios.py`.

## The builder's open items, settled

- **`quartz-fell` (790 km of rail, REFUSE).** I keep REFUSE.
  - Air for shorter distances is permitted only "when justified on grounds of cost-efficiency" (711-712).
  - The lowest fare is 21200 cents, against first-class rail at 12900 (700-701). The train takes 230 minutes, and the
    meeting is the next morning, so there is no time ground either.
  - The signed order says "means of transport as the guide allows". That is the authorising officer's decision
    already taken (2.4.1, lines 645-647), so nothing is awaited.
  - A hold for the officer is not defensible: no sign-off is pending, and the text's test fails on the record's own
    numbers. The new note removes the one real doubt (whether "buys no ticket at all" fits when a train is bought).
- **`dahlia-spur` (the organiser has already issued a ticket).** I keep REFUSE.
  - The signed authorisation states "transport and accommodation met by the organiser; no cost for the agency's
    budget".
  - The staff member may incur expenses only "within the limits set out in the authorisation" (1189-1191).
  - Lines 1201-1204, which cancel the authorisation and enter a new one with expenses, start from the line manager's
    and the authorising officer's own wish. They do not start from the traveller's preference to finish some work
    first.
  - Nothing is pending, so a hold has nobody to wait for. The reason codes (TRANSPORT_PAID_BY_ORGANISER,
    DOUBLE_BOOKING_PROHIBITED) and the six authorised-travel sections are generous enough.
- **`ember-wharf` / `juniper-bay` (exactly 22:00 and 08:00, and "unless the guide says I have to").** Fair.
  - "Before 8:00" and "after 22:00" (650-651) exclude the boundary itself.
  - With the hours rule silent, the guide's default is economy "at the lowest available rates, taking into account
    the times of meetings" (723-726), read with optimum cost-efficiency (1.2).
  - So the guide does decide the flight, and the traveller's "unless" clause is answered. Paying 3800 cents (in either case)
    more for comfort has no ground in the text.
  - "Crack-of-dawn" and "middle of the night" are not rule wording, and both twins use the same words.
  - The one soft spot: sunrise in Madrid in December is around 08:30. That matters only to a reader who puts the
    traveller's adjective above the guide's explicit times, which the request's "unless the guide says" invites them
    not to do. I kept the key; the accept-set additions above cover the codes such a reasoning cites.
- **24 BOOK of 32 (75%, against the brief's "about 26").** Justified. The 32-row mix needs 3 refusal-category cases
  and at least 2 flags (4 are used, 12.5%). The 800 km twin's short side is itself a refusal. Moving a flag to a BOOK
  would weaken the unclear-phrase coverage for one point of share. The share serves the goal: a careful agent
  mostly books.

## Independence from EU Rail

The EU Rail plugin (`handbook_plugins/eu-rail-flight-policy`) was built from the 2026 rail-agency version of the
guide.

- **Requests.** I compared every ENISA request with every EU Rail request using difflib. The highest similarity is
  0.59 (`saffron-dune` against `cedar-mosaic`, both "business if allowed" requests to a long-haul conference). Most
  are 0.37-0.50, and design notes are at most 0.53.
- **Topics.** Some topics overlap because the guides share them: the hours rule, the accompanied Commissioner, the
  flexible fare and the rest day. The ENISA cases rest on ENISA's own text, which differs from EU Rail's:
  - the flat 800 km out-and-back rail test (710-712);
  - business only with a segment of four hours, and no premium economy (730-732);
  - "the outward or return journey" wording (the `hazel-tide` flag);
  - the "manifestly unreasonable" direct-flight test (719-722);
  - departure on validated leave (614-621);
  - the organiser-paid authorisation (1111-1112, 1189-1191).
- **Shared topics differ in facts.** EU Rail's dawn case (`pewter-vale`) is a Paris hearing. ENISA's (`aspen-loch`)
  is a Rome day trip. EU Rail's Commissioner case books Iberia business on a Cabinet request. ENISA's two cases turn
  on the authorising officer's derogation being granted or pending.
- No case is a near-copy.

## Baselines

  - An agent that searches only the trip's own route would also pass `lotus-cairn` (the cheapest Zurich fare is the

- The new accept-set codes add no baseline pass: in `marigold-tor` and `sorrel-heath` the cheapest offer is the wrong
  flight.
